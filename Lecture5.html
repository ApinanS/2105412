5-2 · two real proposals from the running case

The test that kills more can still be the wrong buy

from the previous slide $I=\log_2(N_{\text{before}}/N_{\text{after}})$  ·  $V=I/C$  ·  $N_{\text{before}}=12$
$I_A=\log_2(12/8)=0.58$ bit A kills four, eight survive
$V_A=0.58/0.2=2.9$ price USD 200 is 0.2 thousand
$I_B=\log_2(12/6)=1.00$ bit B kills six, six survive
$V_B=1.00/1.2=0.83$ price USD 1,200 is 1.2 thousand
Verdict B kills more and still loses: six times the price buys about a third of the information per dollar.

5-3 · three ordering rules

Three sentences that settle most orderings without arithmetic

Ranking numbers is fine. Three sentences get you there faster.

Rule 1

Do the free things first

  • Screens, design files, the operator's memory — free.
  • Exhaust them before anyone signs a purchase order.

Rule 2

Cut the big branch first

Splitting the plant in two — air side or fuel side — beats confirming one part.

Rule 3

Leave irreversible last

  • Opening kit, swapping parts, dumping material — each destroys evidence nobody collected.
  • Cheap is no excuse.

5-4 · Woods §6.1.3.1 — one hypothesis, one variable

5-4 · the confounded run, worked out

Zero on the gauge, two opposite worlds behind it

from the previous slide, with gains measured one at a time $\Delta T=(\partial T/\partial F)\,\Delta F+(\partial T/\partial A)\,\Delta A$  ·  $\partial T/\partial F=2.0$ °C/%  ·  $\partial T/\partial A=-0.8$ °C/%
1$\;(\partial T/\partial F)\,\Delta F=2.0\times(+8)=+16$ °C fuel raised 8 %, gain from the clean step
2$\;(\partial T/\partial A)\,\Delta A=-0.8\times(+20)=-16$ °C extra air carries heat up the stack
3$\;\Delta T=+16-16=0$ °C the two contributions cancel exactly
Why zero is the dangerous reading
  • The same $\Delta T=0$ fits “neither valve matters” and “both matter, 16 °C each”.
  • Rooms believe the first and stop testing.

5-6 · failure mode one · ความล้มเหลวแบบที่หนึ่ง · Woods §6.1.3.3 c

Premature closure and anchoring
ปิดคดีเร็วเกินไป และการยึดติดความเชื่อแรก

Two ways a team gets that verdict wrong. First: calling it too soon.

Premature closureปิดคดีก่อนหลักฐานจะพอ
  • Deciding before the evidence can carry it.
  • Symptom: the next step is a repair, not a test.
Anchoringยึดความเชื่อแรกไม่ยอมปล่อย
  • Holding the first belief after evidence killed it.
  • Symptom: “that reading must be wrong.”

Neither fades with experience. The cure is external: criteria written in advance, checked by someone else.

Four biases, and the symptom of each
อคติสี่แบบ พร้อมอาการที่ใช้จับตัวเองได้

These corrupt the evidence before the stopping question is even asked.

most common · about 30 % of cases

Pseudodiagnosticityไล่ข้อมูลที่แยกแยะอะไรไม่ได้

  • Chasing data that cannot discriminate.
  • Symptom: “grab it anyway, it may be useful.”

2

Confirmation biasหาแต่หลักฐานที่เข้าข้างตัวเอง

  • Looking only where support lives.
  • Symptom: you can name support, not a refutation.

3

Availability biasเอาข้อมูลที่หยิบง่าย ไม่ใช่ที่ตัดสินได้

  • Easiest data instead of deciding data.
  • Symptom: only screen trends, nobody walks out.

4

Representativeness biasเห็นความเหมือนที่ไม่มีอยู่จริง

  • A likeness that is not there.
  • Symptom: “just like last year” — before checking.

5-6 · the model behind the stopping point

Two costs move in opposite directions

Too early and too late are the same decision, priced. Here is the price.

$$C_{\text{tot}}(n)=n\,c+p(n)\,D,\qquad p(n)=p_0\,e^{-n/k}$$

Only while tests are independent and equally strong; real evidence does not decay this smoothly.

Test cost climbs with $n$; expected damage falls. Their sum has a minimum.

5-6 · failure mode two

The team that spends the budget without deciding

Left of the minimum is premature closure. Right of it is this.

  • Every meeting ends with “a bit more data”, and nobody says what it would change.
  • Tests are still bought after only one hypothesis is left — that is comfort, not information.
  • The plant runs below target the whole time: USD 600 an hour, charged to nobody.
The question that ends it “If the next result came out either way, would we act differently?” If not, decide.

5-7 · a · Woods case 26, contributed by T. E. Marlin

More fuel, less heat — and the flare has gone black

The plant, and what changed
  • A fired heater preheats a hydrocarbon feed to 205.9 °C for a packed-bed reactor
  • The outlet temperature controller TC-3 reaches the fuel valve and nothing else — no controller moves the air
  • Air is set by hand on the flow controller FC-5; the damper sits one-third closed
  • Chasing a growing market, the operators have raised the feed from 6.8 to 8.2 L/s
The symptom on the trend
  • The outlet temperature droops below setpoint and keeps drooping
  • The fuel valve is already wide open — TC-3 has nothing left to give
  • Fuel flow is 50 % above normal, and the flare is black
Three tempting first moves — calibrate, open the furnace, cut the feedrate. Two answer nothing; the third destroys the evidence.

5-7 · e · Woods stage 4.3c · the action that stops the change
